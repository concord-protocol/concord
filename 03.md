# CORD-03: Channels

A Channel is one message plane inside a Community: *defined* in the Control Plane, but carrying its own chat on an independent Chat Plane (CORD-02). A Community is simply a set of Channels unified under a common Control Plane.

Channels come in two kinds, differing only in *who* may read them:

**Public**: readable by every member. Its key is *derived* from the `community_root`, so nothing is ever delivered and it rotates for free whenever the base does.

**Private**: readable only by granted role-holders. Its key is an *independent* random secret, delivered on grant and rekeyed on removal (CORD-06).

----

## 1. Keying

Every Channel is addressed and encrypted through the one `group_key` derivation (CORD-02); only its *secret* and *epoch* differ by kind:
```
Public  channel_pk = group_key("concord/channel", community_root, channel_id, root_epoch).pk
Private channel_pk = group_key("concord/channel", channel_key,    channel_id, channel_epoch).pk
```
Because `channel_id` is folded into the derivation, every Channel gets its own distinct address regardless of which secret feeds it. A Public Channel is just "a Channel whose key derives from the `community_root`", not a separate mechanism.

A Public Channel needs no key generation or distribution — every member can derive it, it adds nothing to an invite, and a single Refounding (CORD-06) severs it along with the base. A Private Channel needs real independence (a leaked channel key exposes only that one Channel, and it rotates independently), so it carries its own key and its own epoch.

### Restricted Channels

Every reader of a Channel can also write it, since the address and the signer derive from the same secret that decrypts it. A Channel is **restricted** when `@everyone` there, with only its own overwrite applied, `(@everyone − deny) ∪ allow` at the Channel's scope (its Category's when synced, CORD-04 §8), lacks SEND_MESSAGES, public or private alike, and it splits the two the way the Control Plane does (CORD-01, Write-Restricted Streams): posting takes a **writer key** that only its posters hold, while reading stays exactly as above.
```
writer_pk = group_key("concord/channel-writer", writer_secret, channel_id, writer_epoch).pk   // the address; its sk signs the wraps
conv_key  = the Channel's read key, exactly as above                                            // encrypts the wraps
```
The writer secret is 32 random bytes per **writer epoch**. It reaches every member whose resolved permissions there hold SEND_MESSAGES, and every `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder, on the Channel's access plane (§5), its blobs bound to the Channel's **writer scope**, `sha256("concord/writer-scope" ‖ channel_id)`, so a writer blob can never be spliced in as a read key (CORD-06 §1), and each delivery tagged `["writer", "<writer_pk hex>"]` with the address its key claims to derive to. A member adopts a delivered secret, whoever delivered it, only if it derives to exactly the address the Channel's Writers ledger names for that epoch and the Roster lets them post there; a member who left or was removed is no writer, whatever a Role still says. A relay that serves a plane only to a connection authenticated as its key can't serve a writer address to readers, who hold no key for it.

**Writer certificates.** Each writer epoch also has a **certifier**: a keypair its minter makes, whose public key the ledger entry names. With the key, every writer is handed a certificate: the certifier's BIP-340 signature over the id of the never-published event `{pubkey: <certifier>, created_at: 0, kind: 3320, tags: [["digest", hex(d)]], content: ""}`, where `d = sha256("concord/writer-cert" ‖ community_id ‖ channel_id ‖ epoch as 8 bytes big-endian ‖ writer pubkey)`. A certificate rides the delivery that carries its holder's blob, as `["cert", "<recipient hex>", "<signature hex>"]` (at most 48 recipients to an event, so one fits a 64 KB relay limit), and a member takes only a certificate the ledger's certifier signed for them. Every post a writer sends at a writer address carries its own, `["wcert", "<signature hex>"]`, so any reader can check, offline and for good, that its author was a writer at that epoch; a writer MUST NOT post there without it, since such a post could never stand, and drops and collects again a certificate the ledger's certifier didn't sign. A post's certificate is its first `wcert` tag, and a malformed one counts as none. Hex here is lowercase. As a test vector:
```
community_id     01 × 32        channel_id   02 × 32
epoch            3              writer       04 × 32
certifier secret 05 × 32
d                f49b5c7359b09366b5b96e074d2b7c5a84bd714a04bf0e6270d13c02d453e619
certifier        62c0a046dacce86ddd0343c6d3c7c79c2208ba0d9c9cf24a6d046d21d21f90f7
event id         1b6fcc32de4a485d7b0109b0e5dd91b7c676fcb26f7dc41c8c2bdc7e3f2cbe90
signature        cd1c3030e1220c539f1efb50d32e5b83432baa20eaa8709aa935050dc11a6ac6e25c3f9555b729cee65829fdbf1ba5e7656865a4fca860e42b05c8341922895a
```
The certifier's secret reaches each `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder on the access plane at the **certifier scope**, `sha256("concord/certifier-scope" ‖ channel_id)`, tagged `["writer", "<certifier hex>"]`, so any manager can certify a writer the Channel gains without a new epoch; a device that holds no certifier mints a fresh writer epoch with its own. A member who leaves drops every writer and certifier secret it held; its read keys stay, for scrubbing its own posts.

**The Writers ledger.** Which address serves when is a Control entity, vsk 18 at `writers_locator(community_id, channel_id)` (CORD-02 A.6), so every member folds the same answer and a Refounding carries it like any head:
```jsonc
{ "channel": "<hex32>",
  "ledger": [ { "at": <unix ms>, "epoch": <n>, "writer": "<writer_pk hex>", "root": <root_epoch>, "by": "<minter hex>", "certifier": "<certifier hex>" },   // posts move to this address
              { "at": <unix ms>, "stop": "<member pubkey hex>", "from": <unix ms> },                                    // a writer no longer vouched for; "from" optional, ≤ at (below)
              { "at": <unix ms>, "open": true } ],                                                                    // and back to the read address
  "retired": [ [<epoch>, "<writer_pk hex>", <from ms>, <to ms>, "<certifier hex>"] ] }                                // optional: writer entries the cap dropped
```
Entries run in order of `at`, milliseconds like a post's time (CORD-02 §4), writer epochs strictly rising below 2^53, at most 64 of them, so a full ledger fits one 64 KB relay event. Each edition appends exactly one entry to what its predecessor held, verbatim; the first holds one, at version 1. At the cap the oldest entry goes, and a writer entry moves to `retired` (at most 64, oldest dropped first) so its address and window still read, its window ending at the next writer or `open` entry; a dropped `open` or `stop` entry leaves nothing behind, and an edition that would retire the live writer entry is malformed; an entry of a kind a reader doesn't know is carried verbatim and moves nothing (one reusing `open`, `epoch`, `writer`, `root`, `by` or `stop` is malformed, and a new kind must be safe for an older reader to ignore); a retired entry whose epoch named no certifier has four elements, and an address dropped from `retired` no longer reads for a device that never held what it served.

**Judging the ledger.** An edition's new entry is dated within ten minutes of the edition's own `created_at`, and a writer entry names its minter in `by`, the edition's signer. A reader judges each edition along its whole chain, and an edition stands only on a predecessor that stands. Chains grow from the head the reader already holds, and from one root: of the roots an edition signed under `MANAGE_CHANNELS` (held now, or sealed by a Tenure) builds on, a first edition or the head a Refounding carried (whose predecessor isn't served), the one with the longest history, as for every Control entity; between two as long, and between same-version editions at a reader's floor, the lower inner id wins rather than the fork tiebreak (CORD-04 §1). Two managers who restrict at once so settle on one ledger, and a later change of rank never moves it. What a `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder writes here stands, as everything they manage does: abuse of that trust is answered by removing it. A reader refuses an edition that breaks any of this, and one whose new entry is dated more than an hour past the reader's clock (CORD-02 §5's horizon, re-judged as time passes), and keeps the head it had, so a malformed, rewritten or far-future ledger never folds. A head whose signer has since lost standing stays with a reader that folded it, since it records what happened; a reader new to the ledger folds the newest edition still authorized, and the next authorized edition brings the two together.

**Minting and Refoundings.** A minter dates its entry when it publishes, no earlier than the newest post it holds in the Channel dated less than ten minutes past its clock, so a slow clock can't close a window on posts already made; dates the edition no earlier than the entry and its wrap no later than its own clock, since relays refuse a wrap from the future; and publishes only an edition that builds on the head it holds. The edition takes `MANAGE_CHANNELS`. A writer entry names the root epoch it was minted under: a Refounding retires it, and the Refounder, or failing them any manager, mints a fresh writer epoch for the writers who remain, so a member the Refounding removed keeps no way to post.

Writer and `open` entries each hold from their `at` to the next such entry's. A `stop` entry moves nothing: it sits inside a writer window, never an open one, and from its `at` the certificate that window's epoch gave the member it names (a post's author, not an address) vouches for nothing. It keeps doing so after its window's writer entry retires, until the cap drops the stop itself.

**What moves.** Reactions, deletions, voice presence and voice directives (CORD-07 §4, §7) stay at the read address, since a restricted Channel gates posting rather than reacting, a deletion is judged by its signer wherever it lands, and being in a call is CONNECT rather than posting; everything else moves, typing included, so only a writer shows as typing.

**What stands.** A reader judges each post as it arrives, against the ledger, Roster and memberlist it holds then. A post stands when the ledger takes it: at the read address outside writer entries and retired windows; at a writer address while the read address takes posts, since anyone may post then, or when it is dated before that epoch's window ends, carries a certificate the epoch's certifier signed for its author (an epoch whose entry names no certifier is judged on its window alone), and is dated before any `stop` naming its author in that window (one that records a departure counts from it), and before its author went away from the Community while that epoch held (below). A post by a member, not banned, who holds `MANAGE_CHANNELS` stands too, and so does one by a member the restricted Channel lets post dated less than ten minutes past the entry that moved its address, since their client may not have read the move.

**Held posts.** A post that doesn't stand is **held**: the reader keeps its wrap apart from the Channel's history and judges it again whenever its ledger, Roster or memberlist moves, and once it stands it joins the history as a late arrival, never notified. Meanwhile a reader who manages the Channel sees it flagged as an unauthorized poster, never counted as unread, notified, previewed or taken as a sign of membership; every other reader sees nothing. A post dated more than an hour past the reader's clock isn't held, and stays on the relays (a read leaves it until its date nears), nor is a banned author's, and one held whose author is banned since is let go. A Channel holds at most 500 such wraps, 4 MiB of them and 50 from any one author, those dated furthest from now going first; past a Community's 16 MiB, the Channel holding the most gives up its furthest first, so one Channel's flood never empties another. A manager's flagged copy goes with its wrap; held wraps count as read for a catch-up.

**Take-backs.** Whenever a reader folds a new `stop` or writer entry, by whatever read (a backstop's or an editor's included), or its Guestbook records a departure, even one that stands only once a later read authorizes it (from it, in every Channel that keeps a ledger, that member's writer posts alone, never from before its first writer window), it takes back what it already shows, posts and edits alike, dated from that entry, that neither the ledger nor today's Roster stands behind (the Roster's writer vouch read here whatever the Channel is now, so taking back is never stricter than judging). It fetches each wrap again by id and judges it as on arrival, at the address it came to and the epoch it committed, never the window its date falls in (a post that came to a writer address the ledger it holds no longer names stands nowhere; one it can't open for want of a key stays); only one it then holds is flagged for a manager, let go for anyone else, or, for an edit, undone. One dated more than an hour ahead goes too, left on the relays until its date nears; one whose wrap every relay asked answered without, or it has no room to hold, stays, and a read that failed or fell short leaves the take-back owed until it completes. A page of history read on an older ledger is judged again on the one held when it lands, what no longer stands held like any arrival (a manager's flag needs its wrap held), and one stored after the ledger moved under it is taken back the same way, from the first entry the move added or a fork switch dropped (an `open` added takes nothing back). Its own posts it keeps.

**Departures.** For posting (a Kick still stops a writer at the epoch it fell in, below), a Leave, an authorized Kick or a Refounding (which departs whom it leaves out only once every chunk of every snapshot its refounder published on that Guestbook is in: an addendum never stands in for its base) is undone only by a newer Join: no post, edit or reaction since is a sign of presence, nor is a Grant, and a post or edit at a writer address is none at all, since one who left still holds a key to post there. A member who left or was removed is no writer or manager, whatever a Role still says. This reading of membership is for posting and its backstops only; whether an edition is authorized stays the Roster's alone. The writer vouch for a post in flight reads today's Roster only while the Channel is restricted, so a reader that first judges such a post after a reopen holds it. Typing and other signals of the moment that don't stand are dropped.

So a writer's past posts stay theirs whoever later demotes them, and nothing new reaches members through a key someone kept or was handed. What stands at the read address outside writer windows is judged by the Roster as it stands, exactly as in an open Channel (CORD-04 §8).

**Residuals.** Backdating into a closed window, or a manager backdating an edition and its entry together, is the same attributable residual every epoch carries (CORD-06): an entry never predates the one before it, and its edition names who signed it. What else remains is accepted. A permission holder's acts are theirs to answer for, so a Kick is judged on today's Roster and a manager's clock is trusted. A kicked writer who posts only once their epoch has closed, backdated into it, stands for a reader who never read the Guestbook that records the Kick. Past its cap a ledger carries no more stops, and the window alone holds. A held post past its author's share is let go for good. A reader who joins after a Refounding learns nothing of a member's earlier time away (only Kicks are carried on the ledger), so it may stand what a leaver posted while gone.

**Moving.** A minter reads the memberlist whole first, since whom it leaves out is final, builds every delivery, reads the Control Plane again, as deep as its own follow reads it (it stands down only for an edition a reader would fold over the head it holds, by every rule a reader applies: the Roster, the banlist, Tenure, citations and the ledger's own; a read that stops short of what it tracks is no verdict, and it waits), then publishes the ledger edition, then the deliveries, retrying each, so a manager who sees the ledger moved stands down, and a mint that loses its race sends nothing. Each event of a minting delivery carries `["mint", "<part>", "<parts>"]`, counted from 1, and an event without it is never part of one: "the minting delivery left them out" is judged only once every part is seen; nobody mints over an epoch for writers it hasn't served until ten minutes after its entry.

**Restricting and reopening.** When a Channel becomes restricted, the member who made the change mints writer epoch `n + 1` (one above the ledger's highest, 1 for the first), delivers it, and appends its entry; when it opens again, they append `open`.

**Stops.** A writer who loses SEND_MESSAGES there, or is banned, is **stopped**: the manager appends a `stop` naming them, and no key moves, since a key alone lets nobody post (a post needs its certificate). One who leaves the Community needs no stop line: the Guestbook every reader holds records it (an event, never an absence from a memberlist). Nothing they post while away stands. A Leave of their own ends at their next Join. An authorized Kick that falls while a writer epoch holds stops them in every restricted Channel's epoch at once: as with a stop, nothing they post at that epoch from the Kick stands, whatever Join follows, and back, they post again on a fresh epoch. Before any mint or reopen closes an epoch, and once the memberlist is read whole, its minter stops there, from the Kick, each member kicked while it held whose posts sent with its key this device has seen, so a reader who never reads that Guestbook, as one who joins after a Refounding, learns it from the ledger (`"from"` on the stop, earlier than its own date; a reader that doesn't implement `from` applies the stop from its own date).

**Fresh epochs.** A change that loses several writers at once, a writer let back in after a stop in the live epoch or after its whole minting delivery left them out, a stop that would retire the live writer entry, and a manager losing `MANAGE_CHANNELS` or leaving while it holds the live certifier take a fresh epoch instead (a writer the ledger already stopped needs nothing), delivered to the managers and the writers who remain; a manager who lets someone post while holding no live secret mints one too. A writer stopped in the live epoch posts nothing there until a fresh epoch includes them.

**Serving and certifying.** A delivery serves a member only when it names the address the ledger names for its epoch and comes from that epoch's minter or a `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder. Only a device holding the epoch's certifier hands the key to a writer nobody has served, with their certificate (at an epoch with a certifier, a delivery that carried no certificate for them served nobody), never to one the epoch's minting delivery left out after they held the epoch before; a manager without it mints a new epoch that includes them. Whom an epoch vouches for is its minter and each writer a delivery from the minter or a manager certified; whom it hands the certifier to, its minter and those its certifier-scope deliveries from them serve. A minter that can't post there still holds what it minted, and hands it to writers the Channel gains rather than minting again. A manager holds the key by design, so it is never stopped for holding it, nor for losing SEND_MESSAGES. A holder of the certifier hands it, and the key, to each manager its certifier-scope deliveries don't serve yet.

**Backstops.** As a backstop, a `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder acts after a short random delay, longer the lower its rank, and only after reading the Control Plane again and finding the ledger unmoved and the Channel's permissions as it read them: when the ledger disagrees with the Channel's permissions or a Refounding retired its live entry; when a writer the Channel no longer lets post, whom it strictly outranks, is still vouched for and not stopped (one is stopped, several take a fresh epoch), judged only on a memberlist this device has read whole since it started (a read longer than one pass goes on where the last stopped, stepping past a second that holds more than a page; a Refounding moves the Guestbook, and the read starts again), with the Guestbook caught up again before it acts on one writer or several, and nothing done when the catch-up fails; when someone who no longer manages holds the live certifier; when a writer the Channel lets post again, who doesn't manage it, is stopped in the live epoch, was kicked while it held, or was left out by its whole minting delivery, judged on a memberlist read whole; when the live entry names no certifier; and when it may post itself but holds no key it can open ten minutes after both the live entry began and it could first post. A manager brings one Channel's ledger in line at most once an hour, and stops each writer, or mints for itself, at most once in six hours, counted from when it last tried; a key passed on without a certificate is no one's to stop, so a writer handing it around can't make the ledger churn. A minter retrying a failed publish reuses the secret it minted, never one it was delivered.

## 2. Metadata

A Channel is *defined* by a **ChannelMetadata** entity in the Control Plane (CORD-04), holding its `channel_id`, name, and `private` flag. Every Channel is callable — a call is simply started in it (CORD-07), so there is no per-Channel voice flag. Edits fold as versioned editions like any control state, so a rename or visibility change is an authorized, convergent edit rather than a new Channel. The edition's content (the `channel_id` is the edition's `eid`, CORD-04), plus the optional client-extensible `custom` object (CORD-02 §6):
```jsonc
{ "name": "general", "private": false }                  // name ≤ 64 bytes, the protocol-wide cap (CORD-04)
{ "name": "lounge",  "private": false }                  // any Channel is callable (CORD-07)
```
Four more fields are protocol fields, optional and round-tripped by every editor (CORD-02 §6):
```jsonc
{ "name": "announcements", "private": false,
  "position": "M",          // position key, CORD-04 §3's alphabet: lower sorts first
  "category": "<hex32>",    // the Category it files under (§4); absent = uncategorized
  "topic": "Release notes", // ≤ 1024 bytes
  "view": "voice" }         // "chat" (the default), "forum" or "voice"
```
`position` orders the sidebar: a drag is one edition, on the moved Channel, with a key between its new neighbours; a Channel without one sorts after every Channel with one, then by name, then by `channel_id`. `view` tells a client how to open the Channel and changes nothing on the wire: a `"forum"` opens thread-first, a `"voice"` opens its call (CORD-07) with the Channel's own chat beside it, readable and writable without joining. A client MUST treat an unknown `view` as `"chat"`.

A **Private Channel seals its metadata**: everything but what every member needs to resolve access is encrypted under the Channel's key, the Pin List's sealed form (CORD-04 §7):
```jsonc
{ "private": true,
  "epoch": "4",                                     // decimal string: the channel_epoch the seal is under
  "sealed": nip44_encrypt(channel_conv_key, { "name": …, "topic": …, "position": …, "category": …, "view": …, "custom": …, "creator": "<hex>", "prev": "<hex>" | null }) }
```
A member without the key sees that a Private Channel exists and, through its ACL, who may read it (CORD-04 §8), never its name, topic or place. Only a key holder can write a sealed edition, so renaming, re-filing or moving a Private Channel takes `MANAGE_CHANNELS` *and* the key; deleting it takes only the bit, by re-publishing the sealed content verbatim beside `"deleted": true`, an edition exempt from the `prev` check below and from the re-seal backstop. A Channel rekey leaves the metadata sealed under a key some members never held, so the rotator re-seals it under the new key as a rotation duty (CORD-06), with any `MANAGE_CHANNELS` key holder as backstop after a short random delay, exactly as for Pins; the same backstop seals a Private Channel whose head is still plain. A Community still on the legacy Roster (CORD-04 §2) keeps private metadata plain, since its clients predate the seal. The sealed plaintext names the edition's predecessor (`prev`, its hash) so a sealed edition can't be replayed as a newer one; a reader opening a seal whose `prev` isn't the edition's own treats it as unreadable. A reader holding the key of a seal's epoch that still can't open it keeps the details it last read, and tries again whenever it folds, since the key it holds for that epoch may change; and any `MANAGE_CHANNELS` key holder seals them again, after the same rank-staggered random delay as any backstop, from the Channel as it stands after reading the Control Plane again; a rename, a move and a reseal are written from the newest details the writer folded, after its rekey walk has caught up. A writer MUST NOT publish a sealed edition built from metadata it couldn't read.

A Channel is *created* by minting a random 32-byte `channel_id` and publishing this entity's first edition (`MANAGE_CHANNELS`, the owner at genesis); a Private one mints and delivers its independent key alongside. Since the id is random, a ChannelMetadata edition at another entity's coordinate (a Role's, a Grant's, the Banlist's, any other Control entity's) is aimed at that entity and is no Channel: readers ignore it. A Channel is *deleted* by an edition setting `"deleted": true`. Deletion is terminal, judged over the whole authorized chain rather than the head alone, so no later edition revives it: the id is never reused, clients drop the Channel from display and may discard its keys. Its history stays decryptable to anyone who already held them — deletion, like privatising, can't unshare the past.

Converting a Public Channel to Private is a key rotation: the Channel mints its own independent key at the **next channel_epoch** (monotonic, never resetting — the first privatisation is epoch 1 and each later one climbs), delivers it to the desired role-holders, and flips its metadata flag, all as a single authorized action (`MANAGE_CHANNELS`). The `channel_id` never changes across any conversion; only the key and epoch turn over. Because the epoch only climbs, privatise → publish → privatise is safe: each private generation lives at a distinct epoch, a stale key (always a lower epoch) can never share a coordinate with the current one, and a joiner is simply handed the current `(key, epoch)` in its invite (CORD-05). Privatising protects the *future* only: pre-conversion history was written under `community_root`-derived keys every member holds, so it stays readable to all members.

Converting Private to Public reverses it: the Channel begins deriving from the `community_root` going forward, and a member joining after the switch reads only the now-public history, never the prior private messages (they never held that key).

## 3. Messages

A Channel's Chat Plane carries the ordinary append events (messages, reactions, edits, deletes), each wrapped as a Stream event (CORD-01), signed by the Channel's `group_key` and sealed to the author's real identity within:
```json
{ "kinds": [1059], "authors": ["<channel_pk per held epoch>"] }
```
Clients load a Channel newest-first and paginate backwards, querying every epoch pubkey they hold (a Public Channel across the base epochs, a Private one across its own) and, on a restricted Channel, every writer address its Writers ledger names, live or retired, so history spanning a rekey stays continuous.

Concord Channels make CORD-01's binding a requirement: each message MUST commit `["channel", channel_id]` and `["epoch", n]` inside the author-signed rumor, and a receiver MUST check both strict-equal against the Channel and epoch whose key decrypted the wrap, dropping a mismatch. No member can re-wrap another's message into a different Channel or replay it across an epoch. On a restricted Channel (§1) a rumor served at a writer address also commits `["wepoch", "<n>"]`, checked strict-equal against the writer epoch whose address served it, and a reader drops a rumor carrying `wepoch` at the read address.

Concord distinguishes two ways one message references another. An **inline quote** stays a kind 9 and carries a `q` tag (NIP-C7) citing the quoted *rumor* id; it renders inline in the timeline. A **threaded reply** is a separate action — a kind 1111 comment (NIP-22) with uppercase `K`/`E`/`P` pinning the thread root and lowercase `k`/`e`/`p` the immediate parent (all rumor ids) — and renders in a thread off its root rather than as a top-level row. A reply inherits its parent's uppercase root tags verbatim, so the root stays stable at any depth. Both bind `channel`/`epoch` like any Chat rumor, and reactions, edits, and deletes target either by rumor id (their `k` tag naming `9` or `1111`). See CORD-02 Appendix B and examples §2.1–2.2.

A message mentions a Role with a `["role", "<role_id>"]` tag beside its text, which names it as `@` and the Role's name so a client that doesn't know the tag still reads it; who gets notified is CORD-04 §8's. A message carries at most 16 such tags.

**Moderation hides.** A member holding MANAGE_MESSAGES in the Channel (CORD-04 §8) who strictly outranks a message's author hides it with a kind 5 on the Chat Plane, `["e", "<rumor id>"]` and `["k", "<kind>"]` as any delete, plus their `vac` (CORD-04 §5). Every reader honors it as it would the author's own delete, judged against the Roster as it stands: a hide by someone later stripped of the bit comes undone, the same rule as every authority action. A hide is a reader's courtesy, never an erasure: the event stays on relays and in every key holder's reach.

## 4. Categories

A **Category** groups Channels in the sidebar and can carry the rules its Channels sync to (CORD-04 §8). It is a Control entity of its own, vsk 15, its coordinate a random 32-byte `category_id`, edited under `MANAGE_CHANNELS` like any Channel:
```jsonc
{ "name": "Staff", "position": "B" }     // ≤ 64 bytes; position orders Categories among themselves
{ "deleted": true }                      // terminal
```
Categories sort by `position`, Channels sort within their Category, and uncategorized Channels render first; a `position` that isn't a rank key (CORD-04 §3) reads as absent, on a Channel or a Category. A Category edition whose name runs past 64 bytes is malformed, and a reader folds at most 100 Categories, the lowest `category_id`s of those not deleted. Moving a Channel is one edition, on the Channel. A client shows a Category only while the viewer can see at least one of its Channels, so an empty or all-private Category advertises nothing to a member who can't read inside it; the name itself is plaintext on the Control Plane, so it shouldn't be a secret.

Deleting a Category unfiles its Channels, which keep pointing at the dead id and render uncategorized until someone files them again, and leaves its ACL in place: a Channel synced to it keeps resolving the same rules (CORD-04 §8). A new public Channel created inside a Category syncs to it, while a private one keeps its own overwrites; that is how a "Staff" Category hands its whole set of Channels to one Role.

## 5. Channel Access

Read access to a Private Channel is its key, and the key goes to exactly the members whose resolved permissions there hold VIEW_CHANNEL (CORD-04 §8). Nobody grants channels one by one: a Role, an overwrite or a sync decides, and the keys follow.

**Delivery.** When a member becomes entitled, by a Grant, an overwrite, a sync or a new Channel, they receive every key they now lack. Two paths carry it, and either suffices:
- The member whose action caused it sends the keys it holds in **one** Direct Invite bundle (CORD-05 §6), every newly entitled Channel at once.
- Any member holding a Channel's key delivers what's missing on the Channel's **access plane**, `group_key("concord/channel-access", community_root, channel_id, root_epoch)`: kind 3303 deliveries (CORD-06 §1) tagged `["delivery", "access"]`, one blob per recipient at the Channel's current epoch. Any member can compute any recipient's locator there, so everyone can see who is already served: a holder that finds an entitled member unserved waits a short random interval, checks again, and delivers, so many holders collapse into one delivery. Served means a blob at the Channel's current epoch from a deliverer the holder's fold entitles (anyone can place a blob at anyone's locator), or the rotation that minted that epoch carrying them. A member who held the previous epoch and whom that rotation left out was cut by it, and no holder serves them, whatever a lagging Roster says. A holder delivers only from a whole read of the Roster. An entitled member without the key derives the address from the root and finds its blob, taking only an epoch its rekey walk has reached or the one after.

A delivered key is adopted only by a member the reader's own fold says is entitled, from a deliverer it says holds the key's Channel and hasn't banned. A delivery is never a rotation: it carries no removal verdict, whatever it omits (CORD-06 §1).

**Read Message History.** A member delivered a Channel's key while holding READ_MESSAGE_HISTORY there also receives every earlier epoch its deliverer holds, but **read-only**: the epoch's Chat Plane address (its group public key) and conversation key, never the secret, so they can find and decrypt the Channel's history but can't sign a wrap into any of its past planes. Each event carries one epoch, tagged `["delivery", "history"]`, its `scope` the Channel's **history scope**, `sha256("concord/history-scope" ‖ channel_id)`, and its epoch that epoch's; each blob's plaintext is `history_scope ‖ epoch (8 bytes, big-endian) ‖ address ‖ conversation key`, 104 bytes, so a history blob can't be spliced in as a current key. A reader takes history only from a deliverer its fold entitles to the Channel. A relay that serves a plane only to a connection authenticated as its key can't serve read-only history. Without the bit, history starts where access did: the current epoch's key opens everything sent in it, so such a member is never handed it, by a delivery or an invite bundle. A Channel rekey (CORD-06) that includes them lets them in, minted by whoever made the change that gave them VIEW_CHANNEL when they hold `MANAGE_CHANNELS`, else by any `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder who finds them entitled and keyless.

**Revocation.** A member who loses VIEW_CHANNEL is cut by a Channel rekey (CORD-06) that leaves them out, published by whoever made the change that caused it. As a backstop, any `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder who strictly outranks the member and can see from the last rotation's and the access plane's blobs that they still hold the key rotates the Channel after a short random delay. A writer key follows SEND_MESSAGES the same way (§1). Neither can unshare what was already read.
